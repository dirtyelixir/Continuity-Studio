# Playable performance within the adopted direction

Apply when a scene or shot depends on a character pursuing an outcome, receiving information or concealing a response. Scale to the requested genre, medium and established acting style; a restrained default is not a ban on justified broad comedy, animation or emotional release. This is a reasoning method for existing production fields, not a new output schema or a requirement to add acting to every shot.

## Intention, stimulus and strategy

Ground the performance in what this character currently knows, wants from a specific counterpart and can actually observe. Identify the obstacle and current tactic (for example persuade, test, delay, conceal or refuse). A playable objective such as getting the other person to stay gives an action direction; “look sad” alone does not. Source-grounded interpretation may guide staging, but never establish an unprovided secret, relationship, motive, decision or plot outcome as canon. If an essential motive is unspecified, use the least assumptive action supported by the exchange or report the ambiguity in the existing notes field.

Organize a meaningful change as: existing physical state → received stimulus → assessment → release, suppression or disguise → speech/action → observation of the result → residual state. A tactic changes when the source event makes it fail, supplies new information or changes the relationship; sustained tactics need no artificial turn. Characters cannot react to information before it reaches them. A listener may begin reacting to the decisive word before the speaker finishes, provided the supplied dialogue timing allows it.

## Select visible behavior

Choose a small number of readable signals with a purpose: assessing, leaking a reaction, concealing it, pressuring, approaching or withdrawing. Derive them from the established posture, breath, distance, voice or ongoing task. Continuing, pausing or resuming an existing activity can carry a turn; do not invent a prop or new activity that changes the source or frozen frame. Remove gestures that merely duplicate the dialogue or decorate an emotion.

Match scale to framing: a wide view needs legible posture, distance and timing; a close view may use a held breath, gaze shift or unfinished mouth movement. Prefer minimal facial change when restraint fits the source, with natural onset and residual tension rather than simultaneous full-face expressions. Facial action analysis is an optional descriptive aid, never a fixed emotion-to-expression lookup. Write visible natural-language behavior, not AU codes or microscopic precision. Do not impose a fixed number of beats, signals, pauses or reaction-delay seconds.

Before a key line, make clear why the character speaks now; during it, choose only consequential emphasis or delivery; afterwards, show what response they watch for. Keep all original words, speakers, languages, speech windows and audible/silent intent. Silence can be an active assessment, refusal or attempt to regain control. A listener remains attentive without taking focus from the adopted shot purpose; multiple people respond according to their actual attention and knowledge, rather than moving in unison. Voice-only identities remain audible without invented visible bodies.

## Carry the state through the edit

Carry the effective tactic, gaze, breathing, weight, hand/prop state and any unresolved response across an actually continuous boundary. Strong reactions do not reset simply because the camera changes; a controlled exterior may still retain a vocal or bodily trace. An explicit time jump or source-supported recovery can change the state. Evaluate the chosen edit-out/edit-in moments, not only full generation endpoints, and keep the essential stimulus and response readable in the planned selected range. If the allotted time or framing cannot communicate the event, report the need for a plan revision instead of changing adopted duration, dialogue or coverage in an H3 prompt.

During planning, put concise observable action and timing in existing Shot beats, blocking, expression and state fields as their schemas allow. During H3 writing, execute that adopted performance inside the selected mode's existing contract; do not expose this analysis as extra sections or put shot-specific behavior in a Scene global. Frozen keyframes still describe one instant, not an action sequence. No new reference-image interview, provider choice, generation authorization or media execution follows from this method.

## Review the causal chain

Use the existing review schema and literal source evidence. Check whether the character has received the trigger, whether the reaction serves a supported objective, whether it is visible in the actual framing/selected range, whether listening and speech timing are plausible, and whether a continuous cut preserves the remaining state. Report unsupported motive, premature reaction, contradictory hand/action state, unreadable crucial response or unjustified reset through the existing issue fields; do not require new issue codes or fail a plan merely for omitting named analytical steps. Still images can establish pose and appearance, not prove temporal acting quality. Text review does not prove generated performance.

Example: the source establishes that a character wants a visitor to stay, is already folding a shirt, and hears “I won't come back.” A playable direction may pause the existing fold on the decisive phrase, interrupt an inhalation, then deliver the exact next scripted line with attempted composure while watching the visitor. The next continuous view inherits the unfinished fold and held tension. Do not add tears, a confession, a prop transfer or a decision to leave unless the source calls for it.
