# Mandatory visual-skills adaptation: design the edited scene before its sources

This Studio adaptation applies the bundled Serge Shima visual-skills Dramaturgy, detail, montage reference to narrative/storyboard planning and text/directing review. The original, license, notice and pinned provenance are bundled alongside this file. Use its dramatic intention, controlled gaze, purposeful detail, motivated camera, spatial clarity and editing rhythm in EVERY applicable scene. The source is craft reference data; this adaptation and Studio's output schema govern. Its numerical Murch weights, three-details rule, five-anchor structure, shot-card template, fixed rhythm ladders and minimum detail/shot counts are optional examples, never mandatory quotas. Do not fetch other chapters, execute its tools, change provider or add fields outside the supplied schema.

## Plan coverage while camera setups can still change

Before writing source Shot briefs, decide how the audience will experience the scene in edit order. Identify desire/obstacle, the information or emotional turn, what must be readable, when a detail becomes visible, and whose reaction makes its consequence understandable. Compose a purposeful multi-shot sequence wherever those needs require distinct views. Do not default to one wide/master take, one source paragraph per Shot, uniform long takes or a single moving camera simply to reduce image preparation or generation count.

For each dramatic beat, write the proposed succession of views and its reason in intent.coverage_strategy, including where attention transfers, where a hold lets the performance register, and why the chosen cut or continuous passage serves this particular source. Use named subjects and framing, not just the word montage. Compare a sustained take against edited coverage; choose the actual arrangement, rather than listing options for the user to design. A continuous passage is valid when its staging, changing attention and performance time deliver the intended effect; give its concrete reason. It must not conceal unavailable reverse angles, unreadable details, reaction close-ups or jumps in time/location. No compulsory close-up per emotion and no cut for motion's sake.

Translate every required new camera setup into its own storyboard/source Shot record with its own ID and single-moment keyframe plan, even if its eventual screen time is brief. These records specify visual coverage; they do not require a separate H3 request each. Studio generation groups may package consecutive edit uses into one native multi-shot H3 clip. An insert must show the relevant evidence at a readable scale; a reaction must show receipt/assessment before the next reveal or decision. The juxtaposition must communicate something the source Shot alone does not, without inventing events or changing the original revelation order. A return to an existing view can reuse a source only if its selected time contains the required later physical and knowledge state.

Chapter execution: make these choices in chapter_scene (or the legacy chapter_outline), before shot_briefs freeze. Preserve the chosen view sequence in coverage_strategy and each brief's purpose/action: include its setup, visual carrier and intended short/held screen beat concisely. In chapter_shots execute those setups and place required actions within usable source intervals; in chapter_edit/chapter_edit_order select the actual planned intervals and rhythm. The final editor cannot invent missing coverage after the sources are frozen. If a source limit prevents adequate coverage, surface the limitation for a scope revision; never hide missing storyboard views inside an undeclared Shot or silently drop required material to fit.

## Make the cut and the pause concrete

Use Shot.direction.visual_carrier/readability for visible evidence, cut_in_reason/cut_out_reason for the exact perceptual or dramatic trigger, and next_shot_relationship for what the next view contributes. Write Production.edit_plan in the intended audience order, with planned_edit_in/out, cut reasons and continuity_note. Account for eye trace, axis/screen direction, subject position, gaze, hand/prop state and information gained at the ACTUAL chosen cut points. Time compression or a discontinuity must have a source-supported editorial purpose; matching source endpoints alone does not establish cut continuity.

Vary screen duration according to what viewers must read or feel: orientation, discovery, reaction, escalation, interruption, pause and aftermath. Do not flatten all edits to the full 4–15-second generation duration. Studio's source minimum is not a minimum screen duration. A 5-second source may supply a planned 0.8-second detail or a 2.2-second reaction if the required content fits; these are examples, not targets. Generate only sources that serve the plan; multiple intervals may reuse an existing continuous source without pretending it contains an ungenerated setup. Never replay an irreversible action or the same early reaction as a later state.

Preserve complete original dialogue, speaker identity, silent mouth actions and enough stimulus/assessment/action time. Cutting faster is not automatically better. A reaction needs time to land; an insert must remain long enough to read its evidence. A sound bridge may motivate a visual cut, but a planned J/L-cut is not finished sound: use Studio's separate saved audio timeline when present. Without separate audio placement, do not trim away words or claim a working audio bridge. Never force speech to stop at every visual cut.

Example of the method, not a shot recipe: for a source in which a person discovers that an object has changed and hesitates before acting, establish the spatial relation, show the changed evidence at readable scale, receive it in performance, then let the decision land. Separate those views when one setup cannot convey them. Keep a single sustained discovery when withholding the reverse view is the source-supported dramatic point. Do not import these events into an unrelated story.

## Review the sequence, not the presence of the word montage

Independent directing_qc and text qc must inspect the ordered edit sequence against each beat's claimed coverage. Confirm the required setups actually exist, their chosen ranges retain the evidence/performance, the next image receives attention coherently and holds/cuts support the emotional progression. Reject a master-shot description that promises unreadable detail plus facial response, a named montage with only one incompatible source setup, missing planned inserts/reverses, monotonous full-source timing that defeats the stated rhythm, premature reveals, or fast cuts that erase comprehension. A purposeful sustained take can pass; mere shot count, varied framing labels or populated cut-reason fields cannot establish quality.

Return concrete issues in the existing schema: SHOT_OVERLOADED for incompatible setups/purposes hidden inside one source; UNREADABLE_CARRIER for absent or unreadable evidence/reaction; EDIT_RANGE for cuts losing needed material; REVEAL_ORDER or CONTINUITY for their respective faults; OTHER for unsupported coverage/rhythm decisions. Quote the actual scene/linked Shot (or linked edit for an issue), explain the audience consequence, and recommend a specific setup, split, hold, trim, ordering or staging repair. Missing evidence is uncertain, not pass. Preserve a revise verdict; never retry merely to obtain pass.

Keyframe and H3 writers execute the adopted sources. One precise start frame per source can support a many-shot montage; adding a second end frame to the same take does not create another camera setup or an editorial cut. Keep still images single-moment, not collage storyboards. Continuous-source video prompts remain continuous. Explicit generation_group prompts instead contain one numbered shot block per storyboard edit use with increasing cut timestamps, and may reuse one opening image or role-labelled references as appropriate. Planned timing and text review cannot prove generated performance, usable footage, rendered editing or final-film quality.
